Wine class in Kenmore Square

The Wines of Rioja

Vinodivino, Kenmore Square

A small tasting class on Spain's most famous wine region, capped at twelve people. We'd taste Tempranillo blends and the white Viura, put an old-school oak-aged bottle next to a fresher modern one, and finally learn what Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva mean on a label. Then, if you're hungry, hand rolls are a short walk away.

6:00
Class starts. A small table, pours as we go.
During
How the French shaped Rioja in the late 1800s, the four grapes (Tempranillo, Garnacha, Graciano, Viura), and what the labels really mean. Then traditional and modern, side by side.
8:00
Class ends. If you're up for dinner, it's a short walk to Matsunori in Audubon Circle for hand rolls at the counter, handed over one at a time with more fish than rice. It's walk-ins only, so we'd head straight there.

A lecture in a bookstore, Back Bay

Three Chords and the Truth

Lectures on Tap at Trident Booksellers & Cafe, 338 Newbury Street

A professor and a microphone in one of Boston's favorite bookstores. Delvyn Case has taught popular music history at Wheaton College for 25 years. He traces the blues from the Jim Crow South to the jazz, country, gospel and rock 'n' roll it gave birth to, and on into punk, metal and hip-hop. Part night class, part bookstore date.

6:30
Browse the shelves at Trident, grab a drink and find two seats with a view of the mic.
6:45
The talk: stories, records and how three chords became everything.
8:15
If you're hungry, a short walk to Lolita on Dartmouth Street for Mexican food, tequila and an argument about our favorite songs. We'd wrap up around 9:30.
